# Atomic Claims — Sebastian Mallaby / The Power Law

### PL-CLM-001
Venture outcomes are highly skewed: many investments fail or return little,
while a small number can dominate aggregate returns.

### PL-CLM-002
The familiar 80/20 expression is illustrative rather than a universal numeric
law; the actual tail varies by dataset, stage, period, and fund.

### PL-CLM-003
When outcomes are heavy-tailed, an investment's credible maximum contribution
to fund returns is a first-order portfolio question.

### PL-CLM-004
Improbable innovations often cannot be forecast reliably in advance and must
be discovered through evidence-producing experimentation.

### PL-CLM-005
Staged financing can match capital to the uncertainty being resolved and
preserve continuation or abandonment options.

### PL-CLM-006
A prepared-mind approach studies an emerging technology in advance, derives
likely problems and enabling layers, and uses that map to recognize companies.

### PL-CLM-007
Prior pass decisions can anchor later judgments; a new round should be
re-evaluated against current evidence and the original pass condition.

### PL-CLM-008
An explicit upside or pre-parade section can counter the human tendency to
understate how large a successful outcome might become.

### PL-CLM-009
Networks affect access, information, recruiting, customer connections, and
reputation, and can create cumulative deal-flow advantages.

### PL-CLM-010
Network advantage can also reproduce homogeneity and bias when sourcing and
judgment depend heavily on face-to-face referrals.

### PL-CLM-011
Historical cases distinguish multiple viable venture-firm models, including
small equal partnerships, activist board support, platform services, and
high-velocity growth investing.

### PL-CLM-012
Firm practices must be assessed as systems; copying a visible tactic without
its incentives, staffing, stage, and network can fail.

### PL-CLM-013
Institutional firms deliberately develop teams, give younger partners
attribution and board responsibility, and retain knowledge beyond one star.

### PL-CLM-014
Venture investors can create value through recruiting, strategy, governance,
customers, financing, and ecosystem connections, but value-add claims require
case evidence.

### PL-CLM-015
The search for grand slams can grant exceptional founder power and weaken
governance, with episodes such as WeWork and Uber serving as cautions rather
than universal analogies.

### PL-CLM-016
Venture performance data are delayed, selected, and opaque, complicating
attribution of skill and identification of a repeatable edge.

### PL-CLM-017
Observed venture skill and access can reinforce each other: an early success
may improve later deal flow, making pure skill-versus-luck separation hard.

### PL-CLM-018
Historical winners and elite firms are selected samples; their stories cannot
be converted directly into predictive scoring rules.

### PL-CLM-019
The venture network can combine market selection with close organizational
support, but it also concentrates gatekeeping power over experimentation.

### PL-CLM-020
An AI system should use historical cases to generate hypotheses,
counterexamples, and evidence requests—not as deterministic endorsements.
