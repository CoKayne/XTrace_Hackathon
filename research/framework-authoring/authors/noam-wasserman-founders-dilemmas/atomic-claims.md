# Atomic Claims — Noam Wasserman / The Founder's Dilemmas

### FD-CLM-001
Founder and team decisions create path dependence: an easy early choice can
produce later conflict when the venture, roles, and resource needs change.

### FD-CLM-002
The framework spans pre-founding timing, cofounder choice, relationships,
roles, equity, hiring, investors, founder-CEO succession, and founder goals.

### FD-CLM-003
The relevant diligence question is not whether founders have avoided all
dilemmas, but whether they recognized, discussed, documented, and adapted to
them.

### FD-CLM-004
Founding timing should consider relevant human and social capital, market
readiness, opportunity cost, and personal constraints.

### FD-CLM-005
A cofounder can add complementary resources and shared burden while creating
coordination, ownership, control, and relationship risks.

### FD-CLM-006
Prior friends and family may have trust but can avoid difficult conversations
or carry personal conflict into the venture.

### FD-CLM-007
Prior coworkers provide more directly observed evidence of how people perform
and resolve conflict, but no relationship type guarantees success.

### FD-CLM-008
Founding teams must allocate roles, decision rights, and an escalation path;
indefinite consensus can become a scaling constraint.

### FD-CLM-009
Founder roles should be evaluated against current and future venture needs,
not only idea ownership, title preference, or initial contribution.

### FD-CLM-010
Very early static equity splits can become misaligned as effort, role,
commitment, strategy, and required skills change.

### FD-CLM-011
Equity discussions should consider past contribution, expected future
contribution, opportunity cost, and relationship dynamics.

### FD-CLM-012
Vesting, repurchase, or other documented dynamic mechanisms can reduce
hold-up and dead-equity risk when participation changes.

### FD-CLM-013
An apparently equal split may reflect equal expected contribution or avoidance
of a difficult conversation; the result alone does not reveal which.

### FD-CLM-014
As a venture grows, founders often need nonfounding executives whose skills
complement the founding team and whose roles, incentives, and authority are
clear.

### FD-CLM-015
Founder strengths and attachment can become constraints when organizational
needs change; founder scalability should be treated as a changing role fit,
not a permanent personal label.

### FD-CLM-016
Investor choice changes more than available cash: it can change board
composition, resource access, governance, milestones, and founder control.

### FD-CLM-017
Friends-and-family money can impose relationship losses in addition to
financial losses and requires explicit risk communication.

### FD-CLM-018
Founder success at product or financing milestones can increase organizational
complexity and the probability of CEO succession.

### FD-CLM-019
Founder-CEO succession differs from mature-company succession because founders
often retain equity, identity, influence, and another company role.

### FD-CLM-020
Post-succession role, authority, reporting lines, board position, and founder
commitment must be designed to avoid shadow leadership.

### FD-CLM-021
Founders may value wealth creation and control differently; financing, hiring,
and governance choices should be interpreted relative to explicitly stated
goals.

### FD-CLM-022
Resource acquisition can increase company value while reducing founder control,
creating a trade-off rather than a universally correct choice.

### FD-CLM-023
Population-level findings on control and value are empirical associations with
scope limitations, not a causal penalty to apply automatically to one company.

### FD-CLM-024
Founder-team diligence should request disconfirming evidence, including actual
conflict behavior, role changes, departure terms, and governance history.

### FD-CLM-025
Founder-risk judgments should remain separate from market, product, financial,
valuation, and legal judgments and should not become a personality score.
