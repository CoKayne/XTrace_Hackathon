# Atomic Claims — April Dunford / Obviously Awesome, Updated and Expanded

### OA2-CLM-001
Positioning defines how a product is different and better than alternatives for
a particular set of customers and supplies context for marketing and sales.

### OA2-CLM-002
Positioning precedes messaging, storytelling, branding, and sales execution;
those outputs should not substitute for positioning evidence.

### OA2-CLM-003
The five interdependent components are competitive alternatives, differentiated
capabilities, differentiated value, best-fit customers, and market category.

### OA2-CLM-004
The updated edition separates pre-work, five core steps, and post-positioning
execution rather than presenting the first edition's ten-step sequence.

### OA2-CLM-005
Before an exercise, a team should decide readiness, audience, offering scope,
champion persona, participants, excluded bad-fit customers, and shared terms.

### OA2-CLM-006
An unlaunched product has a positioning thesis to test rather than demonstrated
positioning.

### OA2-CLM-007
Customer positioning is distinct from investor, employee, or corporate-vision
positioning.

### OA2-CLM-008
For a single-product company, company and product positioning can generally be
treated together; multi-product companies require an explicit scope choice.

### OA2-CLM-009
Real competitive alternatives are what prospects would use or do if the
product did not exist and what appears on current customer shortlists.

### OA2-CLM-010
Competitive alternatives are broader than named direct competitors but
narrower than every imagined present or future threat.

### OA2-CLM-011
Sales and win/loss evidence are more reliable for current alternatives than
an internally brainstormed or AI-generated competitor list.

### OA2-CLM-012
A capability is differentiated only relative to a real alternative.

### OA2-CLM-013
Differentiated value is the customer outcome enabled by differentiated
capability, not a feature list or generic benefit.

### OA2-CLM-014
Repeatedly asking what a capability means for the customer can connect feature
to outcome, but the result requires evidence.

### OA2-CLM-015
Value work should focus on the champion's purchase motivation while keeping
other stakeholders and objection handling distinct.

### OA2-CLM-016
Best-fit customers are those whose characteristics make the differentiated
value unusually important, not the broadest possible market.

### OA2-CLM-017
A market category supplies context and associated assumptions so the intended
customer can understand the differentiated value.

### OA2-CLM-018
An aspirational or novel category can create confusion and education cost; new
category creation is a strategic choice with long-term risk.

### OA2-CLM-019
The 2026 framework distinguishes positioning a lead wedge, platform, suite,
individual product, or company in a multi-product setting.

### OA2-CLM-020
Positioning should be tested through structured live sales conversations with
best-fit prospects; homepage tests alone are noisy and do not isolate it.

### OA2-CLM-021
After validation, a messaging document can maintain consistency across sales
and marketing assets.

### OA2-CLM-022
Positioning should be revisited when product, real competitors, customer
priorities, or market conditions materially change.

### OA2-CLM-023
Future competitors belong in product strategy and roadmap before they appear
on current shortlists; they should not automatically define current positioning.

### OA2-CLM-024
Customer advisory, win/loss, and direct account feedback can reveal changing
purchase behavior.

### OA2-CLM-025
For VC diligence, positioning is an evidence-backed go-to-market hypothesis,
not proof of product-market fit or a direct valuation premium.
