# Atomic Claims — Claire Hughes Johnson / Scaling People

### SP-CLM-001
*Scaling People* is a tactical guide to company operating structures and people
management, not a claim that process is sufficient for business success.

### SP-CLM-002
Foundational operating structures generally become useful after a company has
traction or product-market-fit evidence; very small teams should not install
heavy systems by default.

### SP-CLM-003
Scaling requires repeatable structures and cadences that reduce dependence on
individual heroics while remaining adaptable.

### SP-CLM-004
Leaders and managers should understand their own work patterns, strengths,
blind spots, and effects on others before attempting to scale through a team.

### SP-CLM-005
High-functioning teams combine shared norms with complementary capabilities and
explicit awareness of who is suited to which work.

### SP-CLM-006
Leadership and management overlap but are distinct learnable practices:
direction, ambition, and culture differ from scoping work, matching people,
monitoring results, and development.

### SP-CLM-007
Founding documents should make mission, long-term goals, operating principles,
and team charters explicit.

### SP-CLM-008
Long-term goals provide context for shorter company, division, and team goals,
which should connect to the organization's reason for existing.

### SP-CLM-009
Operating principles should describe authentic behavior and decision rules,
not merely aspirational slogans; they require periodic review and expression in
hiring, reward, leadership, and everyday conduct.

### SP-CLM-010
A team charter can clarify purpose, responsibilities, metrics, major risks,
dependencies, and how other teams should work with it.

### SP-CLM-011
An operating cadence creates repeated stability points such as planning,
business reviews, metrics reviews, leadership meetings, and communication.

### SP-CLM-012
There is no universal correct cadence; leaders should select, repeat, inspect,
and improve mechanisms appropriate to their company.

### SP-CLM-013
Goals and metrics should be recognized, reviewed, and connected to business
results rather than operating process for its own sake.

### SP-CLM-014
Hiring diligence should begin from what the company and role need and continue
through research, interviews, and reference checks rather than rely on
reputation.

### SP-CLM-015
For leaders, evidence about team design, hiring choices, followership, and
reference history can reveal operating ability that a résumé alone cannot.

### SP-CLM-016
Past executive success does not prove adaptation to a new environment, and
even a careful process can produce hiring errors.

### SP-CLM-017
Author-direct hiring signals include learning, outcome orientation, resilience,
ownership, and evidence of how a candidate acted in failure or difficult work;
these remain hypotheses to verify, not personality scores.

### SP-CLM-018
Hiring does not end at offer acceptance; onboarding must help a person
understand the organization, role, expectations, mission, and working context.

### SP-CLM-019
Formal leader onboarding may cover the first months, while adaptation and
performance problems can appear later and require continuing observation.

### SP-CLM-020
The order and mix of hires should reflect the company's present product and
organizational needs while preserving complementary skills and shared norms.

### SP-CLM-021
As organizations grow, managers shift from tactical coaching toward unblocking
leaders and must communicate intentionally, consistently, authentically, and
specifically.

### SP-CLM-022
Managers should behave as curious partners rather than assume they already know
the explanation or solution to another person's performance.

### SP-CLM-023
Scaling through others requires delegation, clear ownership, and room for team
members to develop rather than permanent founder or executive intervention.

### SP-CLM-024
Useful feedback describes observed work or behavior and its effects, invites
self-assessment, and distinguishes an observation from a judgment about the
person.

### SP-CLM-025
Candid feedback should be specific, supportive, and constructive; exhortations
to say difficult things do not authorize cruelty, harassment, or certainty
about another person's motives.

### SP-CLM-026
Young organizations may need lightweight feedback structure precisely because
many new managers do not yet provide continuous feedback reliably.

### SP-CLM-027
Performance expectations should be explicit before review, supported by
ongoing feedback and an opportunity to improve.

### SP-CLM-028
A practical test of a functioning performance system is that formal review
feedback does not surprise the employee.

### SP-CLM-029
For product use, performance evidence must be role-specific, privacy-preserving,
human-reviewed, and checked for inconsistent standards or bias; the public
author materials do not validate automated employee scoring.

### SP-CLM-030
Employee-count growth is not itself evidence of company success.

### SP-CLM-031
For VC diligence, operating systems are best treated as hypotheses about
repeatability, accountability, and execution risk and must be verified through
behavior and outcomes.

### SP-CLM-032
Operating maturity does not independently establish product-market fit,
governance quality, investment merit, or a valuation premium.
