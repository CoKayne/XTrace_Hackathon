# Atomic Claims — Howard Marks / The Most Important Thing

### MMI-CLM-001
Above-average active results require thinking that is both different from and
better than the consensus; mere difference is insufficient.

### MMI-CLM-002
Second-level analysis asks what the consensus expects, how the actual outcome
may differ, and whether that difference is already reflected in price.

### MMI-CLM-003
Investment attractiveness depends on the relationship between price and
underlying value, not company admiration alone.

### MMI-CLM-004
Value and growth are not mutually exclusive categories; long-duration growth
can have value, but its inputs demand superior judgment and humility.

### MMI-CLM-005
Valuation calculations are less decisive than the quality of the assumptions
entered into them.

### MMI-CLM-006
Risk is future uncertainty and the possibility of substantial permanent loss;
observed volatility is not an adequate universal definition.

### MMI-CLM-007
Risk can remain invisible before an outcome and cannot be inferred reliably
from one realized history.

### MMI-CLM-008
Risk control differs from risk avoidance: avoiding all uncertainty generally
also avoids the return sought for bearing it.

### MMI-CLM-009
Superior investing seeks asymmetry: potential gain disproportionate to
potential loss, after accounting for the risk actually borne.

### MMI-CLM-010
Economic, credit, profit, psychology, and capital-market cycles recur, while
their timing and amplitude remain uncertain.

### MMI-CLM-011
Market temperature can sometimes be inferred from observable terms,
availability of capital, risk tolerance, and investor behavior without making
a precise macro forecast.

### MMI-CLM-012
Large posture changes should be rare and reserved for unusually compelling
conditions; ordinary mid-cycle signals do not support confident timing.

### MMI-CLM-013
Intelligent contrarianism requires explaining what the crowd believes, why,
what may be wrong, and what action follows; reflexive opposition is not enough.

### MMI-CLM-014
Bargains often arise where sentiment, forced behavior, or neglect creates a
price/value mismatch, not merely where an asset is unpopular.

### MMI-CLM-015
Patient opportunism preserves capacity for attractive opportunities rather
than forcing deployment into whatever is currently available.

### MMI-CLM-016
Short-horizon macro forecasts rarely provide a dependable investment edge.

### MMI-CLM-017
Decision quality cannot be judged from outcome alone because luck and
unrealized alternative histories affect results.

### MMI-CLM-018
Skill should be assessed through repeatable process and results across varied
conditions, not a favorable short sample.

### MMI-CLM-019
For strategies that require large winners, excessive defensiveness can be as
misaligned as uncontrolled aggressiveness.

### MMI-CLM-020
An investor's appropriate aggressive/defensive posture depends on objectives,
risk capacity, and the kind of edge the investor actually possesses.

### MMI-CLM-021
A market event should first update financing, discount-rate, demand, runway,
and exit assumptions; it does not automatically reverse a company judgment.

### MMI-CLM-022
Uncertainty should be represented as ranges, scenarios, counterevidence, and
unknowns rather than hidden behind a precise recommendation.
