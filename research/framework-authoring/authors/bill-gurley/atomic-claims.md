# Atomic Claims — Bill Gurley Public Frameworks

### BG-CLM-001
Revenue multiples are crude because a revenue dollar's value depends on the
cash flows, duration, reinvestment, and risk associated with it.

### BG-CLM-002
Revenue-quality attributes include defensibility, genuine network effects,
predictability, retention or switching costs, gross margin, marginal
profitability, low concentration, low partner dependency, organic demand, cash
conversion, capital intensity, and growth that can become cash flow.

### BG-CLM-003
Historical multiple examples are descriptive and time-bound; they do not
establish a current valuation rule.

### BG-CLM-004
Incremental margin compares changes in revenue with changes in cost and can
reveal whether growth improves or weakens operating leverage.

### BG-CLM-005
Gross margin, accounting earnings, and cash conversion are distinct; variable
costs may sit below a company's reported gross-margin line.

### BG-CLM-006
Customer concentration and external platform or partner dependency can weaken
pricing power and future-cash-flow confidence.

### BG-CLM-007
LTV is a tactical comparison tool whose output is only as credible as its
retention, margin, cost, acquisition, timing, and discount-rate assumptions.

### BG-CLM-008
LTV variables interact: changing price, acquisition intensity, service cost, or
customer mix can change churn, CAC, and margins together.

### BG-CLM-009
Model ownership and incentives matter; spending advocates should not be able to
alter definitions or mix organic users into paid-acquisition cohorts unchecked.

### BG-CLM-010
Paid acquisition below modeled LTV is not a durable strategy unless the company
has an independent advantage and favorable marginal economics persist.

### BG-CLM-011
Scaling spend can worsen CAC and customer quality; average historical unit
economics do not establish marginal economics.

### BG-CLM-012
Organic demand and retained customer value are useful counterweights to growth
manufactured by discounts, subsidies, or marketing.

### BG-CLM-013
A viable marketplace needs natural pull on both supply and demand and should
become easier, not harder, to grow as liquidity increases.

### BG-CLM-014
Marketplace attractiveness depends on experience and economic improvement,
technology/workflow value, fragmentation, onboarding friction, addressable
penetration, frequency, payment flow, and demonstrated network effects.

### BG-CLM-015
Supplier aggregation alone is insufficient; demand aggregation and local or
segment-level liquidity must be measured.

### BG-CLM-016
A marketplace network effect exists only when adding participants improves the
experience for later participants, and such effects are rarer than claimed.

### BG-CLM-017
An excessive platform take rate can increase end prices, weaken supplier
economics, encourage bypass or multihoming, and create an opening for
competitors.

### BG-CLM-018
A lower or adaptive take rate can increase ecosystem participation and volume,
but the optimal rate depends on the value and costs the platform creates.

### BG-CLM-019
A materially better product can expand a market by changing price,
convenience, frequency, coverage, or use cases; historical category spend may
understate potential demand.

### BG-CLM-020
Market expansion requires scenarios and observed behavior rather than a single
heroic TAM number.

### BG-CLM-021
Current penetration, regulation, competition, substitutes, and network
locality remain counterevidence to expanded-TAM claims.

### BG-CLM-022
A large private round does not provide IPO-grade scrutiny or prove that
financial data, controls, and risk disclosure are mature.

### BG-CLM-023
Investors should reconcile gross versus net revenue, contra-revenue, true
contribution margin, and variable costs before applying valuation metrics.

### BG-CLM-024
Preferred-stock layers and special terms can make stated ownership diverge from
economic payout.

### BG-CLM-025
The last private-round valuation is not a permanent price or realized return.

### BG-CLM-026
High burn plus continuing capital dependence creates refinancing, down-round,
recapitalization, dilution, and liquidity risk when market multiples or funding
availability contract.

### BG-CLM-027
Investors and founders can become anchored to paper gains and resist necessary
repricing or operating change.

### BG-CLM-028
Governance prompts include board legitimacy, control concentration, audit
resistance, non-reconcilable metrics, counsel and jurisdiction questions,
large insider liquidity, overlapping interests, and “new rules” narratives.

### BG-CLM-029
One or two governance warnings do not establish misconduct; concern rises with
multiple interacting warnings and failure to provide ordinary verification.

### BG-CLM-030
The system must request documents, independent verification, legal review, and
human judgment rather than label fraud or wrongdoing.

### BG-CLM-031
No Gurley public framework in this pack independently establishes an
investment recommendation or valuation premium.
