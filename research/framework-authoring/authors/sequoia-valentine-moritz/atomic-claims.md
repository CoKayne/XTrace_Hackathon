# Atomic Claims — Sequoia / Don Valentine / Michael Moritz

### SQ-CLM-001
Sequoia's public enduring-company list emphasizes a clear purpose, a focused
value proposition, and a customer problem important enough to require relief.

### SQ-CLM-002
The enduring-company elements are institution-authored heuristics, not an
additive score, probability model, or complete investment process.

### SQ-CLM-003
The historical one-billion-dollar market reference is time-bound and must not
be reused as a current market-size threshold.

### SQ-CLM-004
Institution doctrine favors markets undergoing growth or change, while market
size must be supported by customer, adoption, price, and expansion evidence.

### SQ-CLM-005
Don Valentine directly described the nature and potential size of the customer
problem as a central question in the Cisco investment.

### SQ-CLM-006
Early shipments, backlog, payment, repeated use, and credible customer
references can provide stronger evidence of pull than attention or narrative.

### SQ-CLM-007
Sequoia's Google retrospective cites superior product performance and external
distribution partnerships as evidence of product strength and user relevance.

### SQ-CLM-008
Michael Moritz described Sequoia as having passed on Zappos multiple times
because of return rates, inventory cost, cash-flow questions, and other
concerns.

### SQ-CLM-009
Moritz described later growth and a clearly differentiated user experience as
evidence present when Sequoia eventually invested in Zappos.

### SQ-CLM-010
The Zappos case supports a revisit question when evidence changes; it does not
prove that repeated founder contact, persistence, or a sector rebound predicts
success.

### SQ-CLM-011
Moritz directly emphasized founder purpose, product understanding, connection
with users, and the ability to explain why many people would care.

### SQ-CLM-012
Moritz described sustained focus or obsession as common among successful
founders, but this is a selected experiential judgment rather than a validated
personality predictor.

### SQ-CLM-013
Founder intensity must be evidenced through work, learning, judgment, and
customer outcomes; demographic, pedigree, charisma, hours worked, or
self-neglect are not valid proxies.

### SQ-CLM-014
Sequoia institution doctrine emphasizes agility, rapid identification of blind
spots, speed, and the ability to adapt as evidence changes.

### SQ-CLM-015
Sequoia's public crisis guidance recommends scenario planning, direct customer
listening, input metrics, and distinguishing durable behavior from temporary
change.

### SQ-CLM-016
Current Arc materials organize early company building around founder, customer,
product, go-to-market, business, and product-market-fit questions.

### SQ-CLM-017
Sequoia's public enduring-company list favors frugality and concentrated
spending, but frugality is not equivalent to chronic underinvestment.

### SQ-CLM-018
The 2022 Adapting to Endure deck links changing capital conditions to runway,
scenario planning, unit economics, and converting product-market fit into a
sustainable business.

### SQ-CLM-019
The same deck warns that a company cannot cost-cut its way to a business and
must continue funding its highest-leverage future investments.

### SQ-CLM-020
Moritz directly advised looking for opportunities that do not fit convenient
industry categories and processing new information before forming an opinion.

### SQ-CLM-021
Category novelty is not evidence of quality; unexpected opportunities still
require customer, product, economics, and market evidence.

### SQ-CLM-022
Moritz described company building as a long journey requiring patience,
exceptional people, and a large market opportunity.

### SQ-CLM-023
Moritz directly said that liquidity should not be forced at an inopportune time
when a company's future remains attractive and stakeholder constraints permit
continued holding.

### SQ-CLM-024
Valentine described bidirectional diligence because financing partnerships can
last many years and founders should investigate the investor as well.

### SQ-CLM-025
The LinkExchange and Zappos retrospective discloses hands-on recruiting,
interim operating support, and relationship continuity as possible investor
contributions.

### SQ-CLM-026
Investor intervention requires founder and board consent, role clarity,
conflict controls, competence, and an exit from temporary operating roles; it
is not automatically value-creating.

### SQ-CLM-027
Sequoia portfolio stories are selected retrospectives and do not supply a
comparison set of failed or passed companies.

### SQ-CLM-028
No item or total from this pack is a deterministic investment score or success
probability.

### SQ-CLM-029
No item in this pack independently establishes valuation, expected return, or
an Invest Candidate result.

### SQ-CLM-030
Institution doctrine must not be attributed to Valentine or Moritz unless the
source contains their direct statement or clearly disclosed behavior.

### SQ-CLM-031
Public profile, social-media activity, and narrative familiarity are not
substitutes for investment decision quality or company evidence.
